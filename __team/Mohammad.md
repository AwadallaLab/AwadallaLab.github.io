Before joining the Awadalla Lab, I completed my DPhil in Clinical Medicine at the University of Oxford. Prior to that, I earned my Bachelor’s and Master’s degrees in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology at the University of Dhaka, graduating with distinction. My early research focused on transcriptomics, systems biology, and computational modelling across diverse biological contexts, including viral latency, structural bioinformatics, and personalised drug response modelling. My doctoral research expanded into computational immunology and single cell multi omics, where I developed machine learning frameworks to study clonal evolution and cellular interactions in the immune system. In particular, I built CELLeBT, a computational pipeline integrating single cell transcriptomics with B cell and T cell receptor sequencing to investigate clonal coupling, somatic hypermutation, and spatial immune organisation. This work centered on understanding how immune clones expand, interact, and evolve within tissue microenvironments. I also built SpatialRep, a novel algorithm to analyse and explore spatial VDJ sequencing and explored the germinal center biology with it. More broadly, I am interested in using deep learning and simulation-based approaches to study cellular interaction networks in ageing and cancer.
